Why the hours slip away

  1. No clock anywhere. Checking the time is what makes it drag. Here the only sign of time is the sky: it moves from dawn to night as you chart islands, not as minutes pass.
  2. The sea matches your skill. Flow happens when the challenge sits just above your ability. Clean solves roughen the sea (fewer givens); slips and hints calm it. You stay in the channel between boredom and frustration.
  3. No stopping point. The next island loads by itself after a few seconds, like the next episode of a show. There is no menu to fall back to.
  4. Instant feedback. Every correct digit pops, finished rows, columns and boxes ripple, and a run counter climbs. With sound on, each run plays a rising scale.
  5. No busywork. Notes clear themselves when you place a digit, matching digits light up, and finished numbers fade from the pad. Your attention stays on the logic.
  6. A voyage to follow. Islands form legs, and legs form a journey home to Ithaca. Progress you can see is what keeps "one more" going.
  7. Rest built in. After each leg of six islands you get a 20-second eye break. Over eight hours this keeps your eyes and focus fresh, so the flow lasts.
